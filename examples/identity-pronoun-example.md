# Fictional VN — Identity and Pronoun Ledger Example

> Identity, English pronouns, Japanese self-reference, and gendered speech are separate facts. This fictional example shows the minimum useful record.

## Core ledger

| Character / identity | English reference | Japanese self-reference / address | Speech evidence | Context or progression | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aster | man, he/him | `アタシ`, `あなた` | feminine endings, poised evaluations, playful tags | More formal in public. Blunt with Mina | She/her pronouns or camp catchphrases. |
| Mina Kurose | woman, she/her | `私`, surname + title | clipped professional questions, restrained politeness | Contracts more under pressure | Using emotional coldness for all formal speech. |
| Hana and Nana | two women, she/her individually, they/them jointly | `私`, `私たち`, names used as self-reference | singular and collective identity alternate by scene | Solo action can expose one sister's “I” | Changing every first-person form to “we.” |
| The Courier | reveal-sensitive alias | source-compatible pronouns only | voice overlaps another character but is not proof | Civilian identity locks only after B1:0144 | Global replacement with the later name. |

## Identity axes

Record these independently for every recurring character:

- current displayed name, aliases, titles, and who uses each
- identity and approved English pronouns
- Japanese first- and second-person forms
- gendered, regional, foreign-marked, or otherwise nonstandard speech
- public/private code-switching and route progression
- earliest line where each identity fact is available to the player.

Speech style alone does not prove identity or pronouns. A male speaker can use feminine forms. A woman can use rough masculine forms. `僕` does not imply youth.

## First-person audit examples

| Line | Source cue | Scene subject | Required English | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1:0082 | omitted subject | Mina acts alone | I | Previous and following actions remain singular. |
| A1:0191 | `我々` | Mina speaks for the bureau | we | Institutional plural, not intimacy. |
| B1:0047 | `私` | Hana is physically separated from Nana | I | The singular viewpoint is plot evidence. |
| B1:0060 | `私たち` | both sisters speak as a unit | we | Explicit collective self-reference. |

An automated I/we search makes an inventory. Check each candidate against the scene, including lexical false positives and reciprocal English constructions.

## Address changes are events

Track changes from title to surname and from surname to given name. Track changes from polite second person to blunt address and from adult self-reference to a childlike form. These changes can express relationship or plot information. Do not normalize them globally.

## Release check

- Every displayed speaker label maps to one ledger entry.
- Every alias has a timeline and viewpoint rule.
- All first-person singular/plural candidates were audited in context.
- Gendered Japanese is reflected through voice, not unsupported English gendering.
