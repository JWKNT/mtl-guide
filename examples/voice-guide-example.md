# Fictional VN — Character Voice Guide Example

> A voice guide must describe repeatable language choices. Personality adjectives alone do not tell an editor how to write a line.

## Reproducible voice table

| Speaker / narrator | Syntax and contraction rate | Diction and address | Emotional behavior | Progression | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mina Kurose | complete, compact clauses; few contractions at work | procedural verbs, surnames, precise questions | displaces concern into logistics | shorter and more contracted when frightened | Making every line stiff or clinical. |
| Ren Saegusa | loose clauses, frequent contractions, comfortable fragments | ordinary concrete words, first names | jokes around fear, then states decisions plainly | loses jokes after the midpoint | Adding slang unsupported by the source. |
| Aster | balanced clauses, evaluative tags, selective contractions | stylish but not ornate; `あなた` varies by intimacy | exposes hostility through amused judgment | public polish breaks only in private | Feminine pronouns, eye dialect, or a repeated “darling.” |
| Archive narrator | long observational sentences with careful uncertainty | sensory and temporal vocabulary | withholds conclusions | stable until identity reveal | Upgrading every sentence into ornate literary prose. |

## Useful English levers

- contraction rate and sentence completeness;
- clause length, nesting, and preferred connectors;
- direct requests versus hedging;
- technical, institutional, ordinary, or idiosyncratic vocabulary;
- questions, fragments, repetitions, interruptions, and self-correction;
- titles, kinship terms, names, and second-person avoidance;
- explicit emotion versus displaced description;
- public/private, early/late, and calm/pressured code-switching.

Use several subtle levers together. Do not build a voice from one particle, one catchphrase, or an invented accent.

## Worked decisions

| Situation | Flat or wrong English | Voice-aware English | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mina gives reluctant permission | “Okay, you can go.” | “All right. Go—but report back by six.” | Complete syntax and procedural framing carry her concern. |
| Ren sees an obvious disaster | “This situation is very bad.” | “Yeah. This is bad.” | Short, ordinary language fits his tired directness. |
| Aster dismisses a proposal | “That is a foolish plan.” | “You really do make the most charming mistakes.” | Amused evaluation preserves hostility without caricature. |

The good line is not freer because it is more colorful; it is justified by repeated evidence and the current emotional state.

## Narrator voice

Give narrators the same treatment as dialogue characters. Record what they notice, how quickly they infer motives, whether they describe bodies clinically or emotionally, and how they handle uncertainty. Blank speaker cells do not imply a neutral house style.

## Maintenance

For each rule, keep representative line IDs and at least one counterexample. Update the guide when a later route reveals genuine progression; do not rewrite earlier chapters into the later voice.
